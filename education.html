A

Address

A string of alphanumeric characters used to send or receive cryptocurrency transactions on a blockchain network.

Altcoin

Any cryptocurrency other than Bitcoin. Examples include Ethereum, Ripple, and Litecoin.

ASIC

Application-Specific Integrated Circuit. A specialized computer chip designed specifically for mining cryptocurrency.

Atomic Swap

A smart contract technology that enables the exchange of one cryptocurrency for another without using centralized intermediaries.

B

Block

A collection of transaction data on a blockchain. Each block contains a reference to the previous block, creating a chain.

Blockchain

A distributed digital ledger that records transactions across many computers in a way that ensures security, transparency, and immutability.

Bull Market

A financial market in which prices are rising or expected to rise, encouraging buying.

Bear Market

A financial market in which prices are falling or expected to fall, encouraging selling.

C

Consensus Mechanism

A method for achieving agreement on a single data value among distributed processes or systems, such as Proof of Work or Proof of Stake.

Cold Storage

Keeping cryptocurrency offline to protect it from hacking or theft. Examples include hardware wallets and paper wallets.

Cryptocurrency

A digital or virtual currency that uses cryptography for security and operates on a blockchain, making it difficult to counterfeit.

What is cryptocurrency?

Cryptocurrency is a digital or virtual form of currency that uses cryptography for security and operates on decentralized networks based on blockchain technology. Unlike traditional currencies issued by governments (fiat currencies), cryptocurrencies typically operate independently of central banks.

How do I store my cryptocurrency safely?

Cryptocurrency can be stored in various types of wallets, each with different security features. Hardware wallets (physical devices) offer the highest security for long-term storage. Software wallets (desktop or mobile applications) provide convenience for regular use. Paper wallets (printed private keys) offer offline storage. Always use strong passwords, enable two-factor authentication when available, and keep your recovery phrases in secure, offline locations.

How are cryptocurrencies taxed?

Cryptocurrency taxation varies by country, but in many jurisdictions, including the United States, cryptocurrencies are treated as property for tax purposes. This means that capital gains tax applies when you sell or exchange cryptocurrency for a profit. Mining rewards and staking income may be taxed as ordinary income. It's important to keep detailed records of all transactions and consult with a tax professional familiar with cryptocurrency regulations in your country.

What is a blockchain?

A blockchain is a distributed digital ledger that records transactions across many computers in a way that ensures security, transparency, and immutability. Each block contains a list of transactions and a reference to the previous block, forming a chain. This technology underpins cryptocurrencies and has applications in various industries beyond finance, including supply chain management, voting systems, and identity verification.

What is the difference between Proof of Work and Proof of Stake?

Proof of Work (PoW) and Proof of Stake (PoS) are consensus mechanisms used to validate transactions on a blockchain. In PoW, miners compete to solve complex mathematical puzzles, requiring significant computational power and energy. Bitcoin uses PoW. In PoS, validators are selected to create new blocks based on the number of coins they hold and are willing to "stake" as collateral. PoS is more energy-efficient than PoW and is used by networks like Ethereum 2.0, Cardano, and Solana.

What is DeFi?

Decentralized Finance (DeFi) refers to financial services built on blockchain technology that operate without traditional intermediaries like banks or brokerages. DeFi applications include lending platforms, decentralized exchanges, stablecoins, and yield farming protocols. These services aim to create an open, permissionless financial system accessible to anyone with an internet connection, regardless of location or status.
