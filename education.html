Cryptocurrency Glossary

Blockchain

A distributed digital ledger that records transactions across many computers so that any involved record cannot be altered retroactively, without the alteration of all subsequent blocks.

Cryptocurrency

A digital or virtual currency that uses cryptography for security and operates independently of a central bank.

Decentralized Finance (DeFi)

Financial applications built on blockchain technologies that enable activities like borrowing, lending, and trading without traditional intermediaries like banks.

Mining

The process of validating transactions and adding them to a blockchain, typically involving solving complex mathematical problems with computing power.

Non-Fungible Token (NFT)

A unique digital asset that represents ownership of a specific item or piece of content, such as art, music, or collectibles.

Proof of Stake (PoS)

A consensus mechanism where validators are chosen to create new blocks based on the number of coins they hold and are willing to "stake" as collateral.

Proof of Work (PoW)

A consensus mechanism that requires miners to solve complex mathematical puzzles to validate transactions and create new blocks.

Smart Contract

Self-executing contracts with the terms directly written into code, automatically enforcing agreements without the need for intermediaries.

Wallet

A digital tool that allows users to store, send, and receive cryptocurrencies. Can be software-based (hot wallets) or hardware-based (cold wallets).

Yield Farming

The practice of staking or lending crypto assets to generate returns or rewards in the form of additional cryptocurrency.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I start investing in cryptocurrency?

To start investing in cryptocurrency, you'll need to create an account on a reputable exchange, complete identity verification, connect a payment method, and then place your first order. We recommend starting with small amounts and thoroughly researching any cryptocurrency before investing.

What's the difference between a hot wallet and a cold wallet?

A hot wallet is connected to the internet (like mobile or desktop applications) and provides convenient access but with higher security risks. A cold wallet is kept offline (like hardware devices) and offers enhanced security but less convenience for frequent transactions.

How are cryptocurrencies taxed?

In most countries, cryptocurrencies are treated as property for tax purposes. This means you may owe capital gains taxes when you sell, trade, or use cryptocurrency. The specific rules vary by country, so it's important to consult with a tax professional familiar with crypto taxation.

What happens if I lose my private keys?

If you lose your private keys without having a backup (like a seed phrase), you will permanently lose access to your cryptocurrency. There is no "forgot password" option or central authority that can restore access. This is why securely backing up your keys is essential.

Is cryptocurrency mining profitable?

Mining profitability depends on several factors including electricity costs, hardware expenses, cryptocurrency prices, and network difficulty. For many individuals, mining popular cryptocurrencies like Bitcoin is no longer profitable without specialized equipment and access to low-cost electricity.

How do I protect myself from cryptocurrency scams?

To protect yourself from scams, research thoroughly before investing, be skeptical of promises of guaranteed returns, verify the legitimacy of projects and teams, use reputable exchanges, enable two-factor authentication, and never share your private keys or seed phrases with anyone.
