A

Address

A unique identifier that serves as a virtual location where cryptocurrency can be sent to and from, similar to an email address or bank account number.

Altcoin

Any cryptocurrency other than Bitcoin. Examples include Ethereum, Ripple, and Litecoin.

ASIC

Application-Specific Integrated Circuit. A specialized computer chip designed specifically for mining cryptocurrency, typically offering higher performance and energy efficiency than general-purpose hardware.

B

Block

A collection of transaction data in a blockchain. Each block contains a reference to the previous block, creating a chain of blocks (blockchain).

Blockchain

A distributed digital ledger that records transactions across many computers so that any involved record cannot be altered retroactively without altering all subsequent blocks.

Bull Market

A market condition in which prices are rising or expected to rise, encouraging buying.

C

Cold Storage

Keeping cryptocurrency offline to protect it from hacking and theft. Methods include hardware wallets, paper wallets, and air-gapped computers.

Consensus Mechanism

A method for validating transactions and achieving agreement on the state of a blockchain among distributed participants.

Cryptocurrency

A digital or virtual currency that uses cryptography for security and operates on a decentralized network, typically a blockchain.

What is cryptocurrency?

Cryptocurrency is a digital or virtual form of currency that uses cryptography for security and operates on decentralized networks based on blockchain technology. Unlike traditional currencies issued by governments (fiat currencies), cryptocurrencies typically operate without a central authority.

How do I store cryptocurrency safely?

Cryptocurrency can be stored in various types of wallets, each with different security features:

  • Hardware wallets (most secure): Physical devices that store your private keys offline
  • Software wallets: Applications installed on computers or smartphones
  • Web wallets: Online services accessible through browsers
  • Paper wallets: Physical documents containing your keys and QR codes

For maximum security, consider using hardware wallets for long-term storage and software wallets for day-to-day transactions.

Do I have to pay taxes on cryptocurrency?

In most countries, including the United States, cryptocurrency is treated as property for tax purposes, meaning transactions may trigger capital gains or losses. Activities that typically have tax implications include:

  • Selling cryptocurrency for fiat currency
  • Trading one cryptocurrency for another
  • Using cryptocurrency to purchase goods or services
  • Receiving cryptocurrency as payment or through mining

It's important to consult with a tax professional familiar with cryptocurrency regulations in your jurisdiction.

What is the difference between Proof of Work and Proof of Stake?

Proof of Work (PoW) and Proof of Stake (PoS) are two different consensus mechanisms used by blockchain networks:

Proof of Work: Miners compete to solve complex mathematical puzzles, requiring significant computational power and energy. The first to solve the puzzle gets to add a new block and receive rewards. Bitcoin uses PoW.

Proof of Stake: Validators are selected to create new blocks based on the amount of cryptocurrency they "stake" or lock up as collateral. This requires significantly less energy than PoW. Ethereum has transitioned from PoW to PoS.

What is a smart contract?

A smart contract is a self-executing program stored on a blockchain that automatically enforces the terms of an agreement when predetermined conditions are met. Smart contracts eliminate the need for intermediaries and enable trustless transactions between parties.

For example, a smart contract could automatically release payment for a product once delivery confirmation is received, or distribute royalties to artists whenever their digital artwork is resold.

What is DeFi?

Decentralized Finance (DeFi) refers to financial services and applications built on blockchain technology that operate without centralized intermediaries like banks or financial institutions. DeFi applications aim to recreate and improve upon traditional financial systems using smart contracts and decentralized applications (dApps).

Common DeFi services include lending and borrowing platforms, decentralized exchanges, yield farming, liquidity mining, and synthetic assets.
