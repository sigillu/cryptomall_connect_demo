Blockchain

A distributed, decentralized, public ledger that records transactions across many computers so that any involved record cannot be altered retroactively, without the alteration of all subsequent blocks.

Cryptocurrency

A digital or virtual currency that uses cryptography for security and operates independently of a central bank.

DeFi (Decentralized Finance)

Financial services built on blockchain technology that operate without centralized intermediaries like banks or financial institutions.

Mining

The process of validating transactions and adding them to a blockchain, typically involving solving complex mathematical problems to earn cryptocurrency rewards.

NFT (Non-Fungible Token)

A unique digital asset that represents ownership of a specific item or piece of content, stored on a blockchain.

Private Key

A secure cryptographic code that allows direct access to cryptocurrency funds, functioning like a password for a digital wallet.

Proof of Stake (PoS)

A consensus mechanism where validators are selected to create new blocks based on the number of coins they hold and are willing to "stake" as collateral.

Proof of Work (PoW)

A consensus mechanism that requires miners to solve complex mathematical puzzles to validate transactions and create new blocks.

Smart Contract

Self-executing contracts with the terms directly written into code, automatically enforcing agreements without intermediaries.

Wallet

A digital tool that stores the keys needed to access and manage cryptocurrency holdings, available in hardware, software, or paper formats.

What is cryptocurrency?

Cryptocurrency is a digital or virtual form of currency that uses cryptography for security and operates on decentralized networks based on blockchain technology. Unlike traditional currencies issued by governments (fiat currencies), cryptocurrencies typically operate independently of central banks.

How do I store cryptocurrency safely?

Cryptocurrencies are stored in digital wallets, which can be hardware devices, software applications, or services provided by exchanges. For maximum security, consider using hardware wallets for long-term storage, enable two-factor authentication, use strong passwords, and keep your private keys offline. Never share your private keys or recovery phrases with anyone.

Do I have to pay taxes on cryptocurrency?

In most countries, including the United States, cryptocurrency transactions are taxable events. This includes trading, selling, converting to fiat currency, or using crypto to purchase goods or services. Capital gains taxes typically apply when you sell or exchange cryptocurrency at a profit. It's important to keep detailed records of all transactions and consult with a tax professional familiar with cryptocurrency regulations.

What's the difference between a hot wallet and a cold wallet?

A hot wallet is connected to the internet (like mobile or desktop applications and exchange wallets), making it convenient for frequent transactions but more vulnerable to hacking. A cold wallet is kept offline (like hardware wallets or paper wallets), providing better security for long-term storage but less convenience for regular use. Most users employ both: cold wallets for savings and hot wallets for spending.

How do I protect myself from crypto scams?

To avoid crypto scams: research thoroughly before investing, be skeptical of guaranteed returns or pressure tactics, verify the legitimacy of projects and team members, use established exchanges with good security records, enable two-factor authentication on all accounts, never share your private keys, be wary of unsolicited investment opportunities, and remember that if something sounds too good to be true, it probably is.

What is a blockchain explorer and how do I use it?

A blockchain explorer is a web tool that allows you to search and view information about blockchain transactions, addresses, and blocks. You can use it to verify that a transaction has been confirmed, check the balance of an address, or explore the history of transactions. Simply enter a transaction ID, wallet address, or block number in the search field of the explorer for the specific cryptocurrency you're interested in.
